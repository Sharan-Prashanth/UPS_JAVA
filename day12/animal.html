Animal Description

This is an animal description page.

The tiger is a powerful and majestic wild animal known for its strength, speed, and beautiful orange coat with black stripes. It belongs to the cat family and is one of the largest wild cats in the world. Tigers are carnivores that primarily hunt deer, wild boar, and other animals. They are excellent swimmers and usually live in forests, grasslands, and mangrove swamps. Tigers are solitary animals that mark and defend their territories. As an important part of the ecosystem, they help maintain the balance of wildlife populations. However, habitat loss and illegal poaching threaten their survival. Protecting tigers and their natural habitats is essential for preserving biodiversity.

The cheetah is the fastest land animal in the world, capable of reaching speeds of up to 100–120 km/h over short distances. It has a slender body, long legs, a small head, and a yellowish coat covered with black spots. Cheetahs are carnivores that primarily hunt small to medium-sized animals, such as gazelles and impalas. They are known for their incredible acceleration, sharp eyesight, and distinctive black tear marks running from their eyes to the sides of their mouths. Cheetahs mainly inhabit grasslands and open savannas, where they use their speed to chase prey. Unlike lions and tigers, cheetahs generally avoid direct confrontations with larger predators. Habitat loss, human-wildlife conflict, and declining prey populations threaten their survival. Protecting cheetahs and their habitats is essential for maintaining biodiversity.
